Every incident

Each one is broken into three parts: what happened, how long until anyone noticed, and the one place time went wrong. Open a card for the detail.

What happened
Cold and hot wallet management had been a mess for years. Coins were quietly drained over a long stretch, until the balance sheet could no longer hide it.
How long to notice
About three years. It was bleeding from 2011 and only stopped in February 2014.
Where time went wrong
No real-time reconciliation. The hole surfaced when the books stopped adding up. Around 200,000 of the 850,000 BTC were later found in an old wallet; the remaining 650,000 took a decade to settle.
What happened
The BitGo side of a multisig setup was bypassed, and the hot wallet withdrawal limit was far too generous.
How long to notice
The same day. Abnormal withdrawals tripped monitoring, but the money had already left.
Where time went wrong
The limit picked convenient over safe. One signature could move far more than it ever needed to.
What happened
NEM sat in a hot wallet connected to the internet with no multisig protection. A single private key was enough.
How long to notice
Hours. Staff spotted the balance anomaly, but the withdrawal path had already been emptied.
Where time went wrong
A hot wallet that stayed online permanently with no mechanism to sweep it out on a schedule. The exposure ran 24 hours a day.
What happened
The cross-chain message verifier let an attacker rewrite the trusted target and withdraw funds to their own address.
How long to notice
Hours. The amount was enormous and traceable, and the funds were eventually returned.
Where time went wrong
Once the permission was rewritten there was no cooling-off period to stop it. Recovery depended on the attacker agreeing to talk, not on any mechanism.
What happened
Signature verification could be forged. The attacker skipped the guardian check and minted assets directly on the target chain.
How long to notice
Hours. Jump Crypto backfilled the gap afterwards, which kept the protocol solvent.
Where time went wrong
No delay between minting and verification, so the assets could be sold the instant they appeared.
What happened
The attacker gained access to Sky Mavis systems, assembled 5 of 9 signatures, and moved the funds in two transactions.
How long to notice
Six days. Only exposed when a user tried to withdraw 5,000 ETH and could not.
Where time went wrong
An allowlist opened in November 2021, due to expire in December, was still alive in March 2022. That was the fifth signature.
What happened
One upgrade initialised the trust root to zero. Anyone could submit an unverified message and withdraw someone else's deposit.
How long to notice
Hours after the flaw went public. The copy-paste crowd moved faster than the team.
Where time went wrong
The upgrade shipped without a step to verify state afterwards. Once public, a few minutes was enough to drain it.
What happened
IAVL proof verification was bypassed, and the attacker used forged proofs to mint cross-chain assets out of thin air.
How long to notice
Hours. The chain was paused in an emergency, which prevented a wider spill.
Where time went wrong
The pause was a reaction. For as long as the verification logic was broken, anyone could hit it again.
What happened
The freshly shipped and already reviewed donateToReserves path lacked a health check, which made it usable to manufacture bad debt.
How long to notice
Fully returned after 21 days. On-chain identity was traceable and the negotiation window was taken.
Where time went wrong
Getting it back was the exception, not the mechanism: the attacker did not use a mixer, and the attacker chose to talk.
What happened
The reentrancy lock in a particular Vyper version was broken. Several pools were exposed at once and hit by the same method.
How long to notice
Days. Whitehats and attackers were both on the scene, splitting where the funds went.
Where time went wrong
Compiler versions had no mandatory re-review. The same defect sat on-chain for years.
What happened
An npm publishing credential was phished, and a malicious build was pushed to the wallet-connect component of dozens of DApps.
How long to notice
About five hours. The actual drain window was under two.
Where time went wrong
Smallest amount, widest blast radius: one credential fell and everyone was hit at once. SRI and version pinning stop this class.
What happened
A Safe developer workstation was compromised and the frontend JS swapped: signers saw a normal transfer while signing away control of the wallet.
How long to notice
17 days of preparation (breach 4 Feb, malicious contract 18 Feb, JS swap 19 Feb, signature 21 Feb); the backdoor was wiped two minutes after the drain.
Where time went wrong
Not one private key leaked and not one multisig threshold was bypassed. For all 17 days, nobody on the defensive side was disturbed once.
What happened
A DevOps engineer's laptop was compromised, then a service container, then unauthorised withdrawals. No private key leak found.
How long to notice
Minutes. TRM traced $6.36B across 10 chains, most of it moved within 20 minutes.
Where time went wrong
About $3B was frozen (Tether $1.57B + Circle $0.8B + Ripple $0.2B, roughly 47%), because issuers chose to cooperate, not because of anything on-chain. The industry-wide recovery rate is 8.4%.

Time is not the backdrop here. It is an independent variable that does its own damage, six separate times.

This is not a bug in one protocol. It is the design choice at the core of the ERC-20 approval model: so that a DApp does not need your signature on every transfer, the standard decoupled permission from use entirely. The cost of signing is instantaneous. The risk it creates is permanent.

Of all those failures, which ones are yours to fix

Three of the six sit with attackers or institutions. The other three sit in your own wallet, and they are all the same thing: you do not know what state you are in right now.

Ronin: a four-month-old allowlist never removedA permission outlived its purpose
The check dates every approval and puts “how long ago the oldest one was signed, and to whom” where you cannot miss it. You do not need to remember. The chain already does.
Binance: $6.36B gone in 20 minutesNo time to react afterwards
The check answers one number: how much money is sitting in someone else's hands right now. Not a forty-row list, but an amount you can act on immediately.
Ronin: found by a user six days laterDetection left to chance
The check turns detection into something you do on purpose, in ten seconds. Re-scanning the same address is served from cache for ten minutes, so you can look whenever you want.
Bybit: one second of signing, control lostRevoking cost too much, so nobody did it
The check reduces revoking to one click: a single approve(spender, 0) call, or a queue by “all high-risk / all unlimited / older than two years”, confirmed one by one.

What these fifteen years have in common is not clever attackers. It is a permission that lived too long. Ronin's allowlist lived four months past its purpose. That laptop at Binance lived for who knows how long. And one of your approvals may already be three years old.
